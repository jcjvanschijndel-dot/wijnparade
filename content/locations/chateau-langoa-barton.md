description_en: >-
  When Hugh Barton went looking for his own estate in the Médoc in
  1821, what caught his eye wasn't just the vineyards but the house
  itself: an elegant eighteenth-century "chartreuse", a low,
  single-storey manor built in 1758 by a certain Monsieur de Pontet.
  The estate was then still called Pontet-Langlois; Barton gave it his
  own name. Since 1855 a Grand Cru Classé, Langoa Barton has stayed in
  the same family ever since — in 1983 Anthony Barton inherited both
  this estate and the neighbouring Léoville Barton, a record for
  single-family ownership anywhere in the Médoc.


  A visit takes you through the cellars and ends with a tasting within
  sight of the very chartreuse that seduced Hugh Barton two centuries
  ago. Several types of visits and tastings can be booked through the
  estate's own website. Recommended for anyone wanting to combine
  Saint-Julien with a piece of family history unmatched anywhere in
  the Médoc.
